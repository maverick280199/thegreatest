1.  "Jiska 12 saal se kabza, wahi maalik." 47 lakh logon ne ye reel dekhi.
2.  Ye aadha sach hai. Aur aadha sach, kanoon mein sabse khatarnak hota hai.
3.  Haan, Limitation Act ka Article 65 — 12 saal ki limit deta hai.
4.  Lekin kabza kaisa hona chahiye? Yahin kahani palat jaati hai.
5.  Pehla — kabza khula ho. Chhupkar nahi.
6.  Doosra — lagaataar. Poore 12 saal, bina break ke.
7.  Teesra — maalik ke khilaaf. Khule aam "ye zameen meri hai" kehkar.
8.  Isliye kiraayedaar, ya ijazat se rehne wala rishtedaar — sirf saal ginkar maalik nahi ban sakta.
9.  Sarkari zameen par? 12 nahi, 30 saal. Article 112.
10. Aur saboot ka poora bojh kabza karne wale par hai. Supreme Court zyaadatar aise daave thukra deta hai.
11. Toh agar aap maalik ho: zameen par nazar rakho, kiraaye ka agreement likhit rakho, aur kabza ho toh der mat karo.
12. Ye reel us dost ko bhejo, jiska plot khaali pada hai.

Legal check:
- Limitation Act 1963, Art. 65 (12 years, suit for possession based on title); Art. 112 (30 years, suits by Central/State Govt); s.27 (extinguishment of right).
- Possession must be open, continuous, hostile, with knowledge of the owner; burden on claimant — Karnataka Board of Wakf v. Govt of India (SC, 2004); claim failed in Hemaji Waghaji Jat (SC, 2008).
- Permissive possession (tenant/licensee/relative allowed in) is not adverse unless hostile title is openly asserted — settled principle.
- Ravinder Kaur Grewal v. Manjit Kaur (SC, 2019): a person who has perfected title by adverse possession can sue as plaintiff (not used in script; for comments).
- "zyaadatar thukra deta hai" — Law Commission (2023) note: SC has decided the overwhelming majority of cases against adverse-possession claims.
